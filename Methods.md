Methods
Diel carbon isotope modelling
We developed a mechanistic coupled Python–PHREEQC biogeochemical model to simulate 24-h variations in the stable carbon isotope composition of dissolved inorganic carbon (δ13C-DIC) in a shallow, unstratified lake. The model integrates three carbon-cycling processes — atmospheric CO2 exchange, gross primary productivity (GPP), and ecosystem respiration (ER) — with thermodynamically consistent carbonate speciation and equilibrium isotope fractionation computed by the USGS geochemical modelling code PHREEQC (version 3; Parkhurst and Appelo, 2013) via the IPhreeqc COM interface (Charlton and Parkhurst, 2011) and the Python wrapper phreeqpy (v0.6.0). Input data were linearly interpolated to hourly resolution for model integration.
Isotope-enabled PHREEQC database
Building on the individual-component approach of Thorstenson and Parkhurst (2002, 2004), we constructed a custom thermodynamic database in which carbon-13 is defined as a separate thermodynamic component [13C] parallel to bulk carbon C. Each ¹³C aqueous species ( [13C]O2, H[13C]O3−, [13C]O32−, and associated Ca–Na complexes) parallels its ¹²C counterpart but with fractionation-adjusted equilibrium constants. Temperature-dependent equilibrium fractionation was estimated by adusting the constant (A1) and reciprocal-temperature (A3) coefficients of the analytic log K expression:
log K(T) = A₁ + A₂T + A₃/T + A₄ log T + A₅/T²
For the CO2(aq)/CO32− reaction, ΔA1 = +0.01021 and ΔA3 = −4.284 reproduce the fractionation ε(CO2–CO3, T) = −9866/T + 23.52‰ (T in K), consistent with Mook et al. (1974) and Deines et al. (1974). For the HCO3−/CO32− reaction, ΔA1 = −0.000261 reproduces ε(HCO3–CO3) ≈ −0.6‰. Verification runs confirmed recovery of expected species-specific fractionation within 0.1‰ across the measured temperature range (29.5–33.2 °C). δ13C values were computed from the ¹³C/¹²C ratio relative to the VPDB reference value RVPDB = 0.011237 (Coplen, 2011). Total DIC was partitioned into ¹²C and ¹³C inputs based on measured δ13C-DIC. The Calcite phase (CaCO3 = Ca2+ + CO32−; log K = −8.48) was included with measured Ca2+, and the calcite saturation index (SICc = log(IAP/Ksp)) was computed at each time step. Measured Na+ was used for PHREEQC charge balance (adjusted to absorb unmeasured ions such as K+, Mg2+, SO42−); Ca2+ and Cl− were fixed at measured values.
Sunrise and sunset times were computed using standard astronomical algorithms via the Python astral library. Photosynthetically active radiation (PAR, W m−2) was estimated from solar geometry using a clear-sky model with Bouguer–Lambert atmospheric extinction:
PAR(t) = I₀ · f_PAR · τ^m(t) · sinθ(t)
where I0 = 1361 W m−2 is the solar constant, fPAR = 0.45 is the PAR fraction of broadband solar irradiance, τ = 0.72 is the clear-sky atmospheric transmittance, θ is the solar elevation angle, and m is the optical air mass (Kasten and Young, 1989). GPP was parameterised using a Michaelis–Menten light-response model with a Q10 temperature dependence:
GPP(t) = GPP_max · [PAR(t)/(Iₖ + PAR(t))] · Q₁₀,GPP^((T(t)−T_ref)/10)
where GPPmax is the light-saturated rate at reference temperature Tref (the mean observation-period temperature) and Ik is the half-saturation PAR.
Ecosystem respiration was modelled with a Q10 temperature dependence to capture the observed diel ER variation driven by the diurnal temperature cycle:
ER(t) = ER_base · Q₁₀,ER^((T(t)−T_ref)/10)
where ERbase is the respiration rate at Tref and Q10,ER is the corresponding temperature sensitivity. Unlike the constant ER assumption commonly used in short-term diel studies, the Q10 formulation accounts for the 3.7 °C temperature range observed over the diel cycle at this site.
Carbon and isotope mass balance
The model tracks δ13C-DIC evolution using a carbon mass-balance closure that constrains gas exchange to be self-consistent with observed DIC changes:
F_gas(t) = F_ER(t) − F_GPP(t) − ΔDIC/Δt
Biological C fluxes (mg C L−1 h−1) were converted from O2 rates using a photosynthetic/respiratory quotient of 1.0 (molar CO2:O2 = 12.011/32.00). The isotope mass balance at each time step i is:
DIC_i · δ¹³C_i = DIC_{i-1} · δ¹³C_{i-1} + F_ER·δ¹³C_org·Δt − F_GPP·δ¹³C_fix·Δt − F_gas·δ¹³C_gas·Δt
The δ13C of photosynthetically fixed carbon was modelled as mixed CO2(aq)/HCO3− uptake weighted by the pH-dependent speciation:
δ¹³C_fix = f_CO₂(δ¹³C_CO₂ + ε_p) + f_HCO₃(δ¹³C_HCO₃ + ε_p)
where εp is the photosynthetic fractionation factor and species-specific δ13C values were obtained directly from the PHREEQC ¹³C equilibrium speciation. During net outgassing (Fgas > 0), the δ13C of evaded CO2 was taken as δ13CCO₂(aq) + εkin, with εkin = −0.8‰ for kinetic fractionation (Zhang et al., 1995). During ingassing, atmospheric CO2 (δ13Catm = −8.5‰) entered with equilibrium fractionation εeq(CO2(g)–CO2(aq)) = −373/T + 0.19‰ (Zhang et al., 1995). The gas-exchange isotope-effect magnitude was scaled by a dimensionless parameter kgas,iso.
Gas transfer velocities were converted using Schmidt-number ratios (Wanninkhof, 2014): k600 = KO₂·(600/ScO₂)−0.5. Dissolved CO2 equilibrium concentrations were calculated using the temperature-dependent solubility of Weiss (1974) for an atmospheric CO2 concentration of  425 ppm.
Parameter estimation
Eight model parameters — GPPmax, Ik, Q10,GPP, ERbase, Q10,ER, εp, δ13Corg, and kgas,iso — were estimated by bounded differential evolution (Storn and Price, 1997; scipy.optimize.differential_evolution) using a tri-objective cost function:
J = RMSE(δ¹³C) + w_GPP·RMSE(GPP) + w_ER·RMSE(ER) + Σ_k λ_k[(θ_k − μ_k)/σ_k]²
Weights wGPP = 0.05 and wER = 1.0 normalise the GPP and ER residuals to the δ13C scale (observed ranges: δ13C ≈ 1.4‰, GPP ≈ 30 mg O2 L−1 h−1, ER ≈ 1.1 mg O2 L−1 h−1). GPP observations at 18:00 were excluded from the GPP objective because those 4-h averages straddle sunset. Gaussian penalty terms implemented Bayesian-style informative priors (Table 1) centred on literature values: εp −20 ± 5‰ (Bade et al., 2006), δ13Corg −28 ± 3‰ (Kendall et al., 2001), Q10,GPP and Q10,ER 2.0 ± 0.5, and kgas,iso 1.0 ± 0.5. Optimisation used a population of 30, mutation constant F ∈ [0.5, 1.5], and crossover probability CR = 0.9, terminating at tolerance 10−7 or after 500 generations. Model fit was evaluated by RMSE and coefficient of determination (R2) against observations.
Implementation
The model is implemented in Python 3.12 with NumPy (v1.26), pandas, SciPy (v1.11), matplotlib, astral (v3.2), and phreeqpy (v0.6.0). PHREEQC speciation is pre-computed once for all hourly time steps before optimisation, enabling efficient parameter search. Complete source code, the isotope-enabled PHREEQC database, and example datasets are provided as a Jupyter notebook.
